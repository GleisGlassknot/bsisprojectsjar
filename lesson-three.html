[LESSON 3] TYPES OF REASONING



[REASONING]

It is the process of drawing conclusions based from assumed facts.

Reasoning plays a crucial role in problem-solving by providing a structured approach to analyzing and solving complex problems.

It allows individuals to break down a problem into smaller, more manageable parts, identify patterns and relationships, and apply logical reasoning to reach a solution.


1. DEDUCTIVE REASONING


It is the process of reaching 'specific conclusion' by applying general idea called "premise".

An inference is valid if its conclusion follows logically from its premises, meaning that it is impossible for the premises to be true and the conclusion to be false.


[Example Scenarios]

A.
All men are mortal. → premise

Socrates is a man. → second premise

Therefore, Socrates is mortal. → conclusion


B.
All even numbers are divisible by 2. (True)

22 is an even number. (True)

Therefore, 22 is divisible by 2. (True)


C.
All numbers that ends with 0 is divisible by 10. (True)

155 ends with a non-zero number. (True)

Therefore, 155 is not divisible by 10. (True)


D.
All musical instruments makes sounds. (True)

Jeepneys makes sounds. (True)

Therefore, Jeepneys are musical instruments. (False; not a valid deductive reasoning)



2. INDUCTIVE REASONING


It is the process of drawing a "general conclusion" by examining 'specific obsevations'.

Unlike deductive reasoning, where the conclusion is certain, given the premises are correct, inductive reasoning produces conclusions that are at best probable, given the premises provided.


[Example Scenarios]

A.
Christian jogged yesterday. → True Premise

Christian jogged today. → True Premise

Therefore, Christian will probably also jog tomorrow. → Probable Conclusion


B.
The teacher used PPT in the last few classes. → True Premise

Therefore, the teacher will likely use PPT tommorow. → Probable Conclusion



C.
It's been raining for 5 days straight. → Specific Observation

Therefore, it will also likely rain for the rest of the week. → Probable Conclusion



D.
The flamingos here are all pink. → Specific Observation

All flamingos I’ve ever seen are pink. → Specific Observation

Therefore, All flamingos must be pink. → General Conclusion
